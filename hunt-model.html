Hunt Model

The Hunt Score estimates how likely a player is to go over or under a sportsbook's line tonight. This page says how it works, what it can't see, and how it has actually done, so you can decide how much weight to give it. It is a research tool, not betting advice.

What the score means

For every points, rebounds, assists, 3-pointers and PRA line on games that haven't started, the model plays the game out 2,000 times: how many minutes the player gets, how much he produces per minute, and how much a normal night varies around that. The score is the share of those games that finish on one side of the line.

A score of 65 is the model saying "about a 65% chance". It is a chance, not a promise. See "How it has done" below: so far the high scores have been more confident than the results.

How a score is built

It starts from the player's season-long numbers, then adds one step at a time. Each step shows how many points it moved the score, on the Hunt Score page.

  1. Season baseline. His rate per minute and his minutes over the season (last season's numbers until he has about 20 games this year), against an average opponent.
  2. Minutes. What he has actually been playing lately, with a wider range if he is a game-time decision.
  3. Recent form. Recent games count more: a game's weight halves every five games.
  4. Role. If teammates who normally play 15+ minutes are out, it looks at how he did in past games without them, and how much those teammates matter for this stat.
  5. Matchup. How much of this stat the opponent allows compared with the league, taking half of the gap.
  6. Zone matchup. For points and 3-pointers: where he shoots from against how well the opponent defends those spots.
  7. Pace and back-to-back. A faster game means more chances; the second night of a back-to-back costs about 3%.
  8. The book's line. The projection is pulled part of the way toward the line, because the book often knows something the box scores don't, such as a role change.
  9. Uncertainty. Fewer games, swinging recent games or a game-time decision all widen the range before it counts the over and under results.

What it can't see

  • News after the scores were made: a late injury, a lineup change or a player resting.
  • Who is guarding whom. The data has no matchup-by-matchup defense, only team-level numbers.
  • Blowouts and coaching choices that change minutes mid-game.
  • Anything with a small sample: early in the season, or a player who just changed teams or roles.
  • What the sportsbooks know. The line itself is often a better guess than the model, which is why the model leans on it.

How it has done

As of Nov 8, 2026: six graded nights (Oct 28 – Nov 7), about 4,600 scored sides, checked against the real box scores. This is a small sample, so read the direction, not the decimals.

  • The percentages are overconfident right now. A score of 65 has behaved more like 55–57%. We're working on this and not hiding it.
  • Big "edges" over the book's line have not done better than small ones (an edge of 10+ won 48%).
  • Lines in general went under 53% of the time on these nights, and most high scores are unders, so part of that record is just that tilt.
  • We have not yet shown that the ranking beats a simple baseline. That is why this is a beta.
